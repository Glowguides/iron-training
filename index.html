THE FULL PICTURE

Twelve weeks.
Keep the long view.

Use these planned loads as ceilings; use the adjusted loads in Train. Log each top set to earn an increase. Apply the effort rules after every top set. Lower-lift numbers apply only after the pain check.

Adjust the next exposure

Complete all reps, pain-free: RPE ≤7 → add 10 kg; RPE >7 and ≤8 → add 5 kg. RPE >8 → repeat. RPE ≥9 or missed reps → repeat. Never exceed the week's listed ceiling.

After two consecutive stalls on the same lift, reduce that lift by 10% (round down to 5 kg) and rebuild. Deloads and tests do not count as progression exposures. An unlogged or substituted session earns no increase.

Keep the block moving

  • Miss one session: do it next; keep Day 1–4 in order. Do not double sessions. Leave a full rest day between Upper A and Upper B, including across weeks.
  • Miss a week: repeat the last completed week with 10% less load and half the accessory sets. Resume only after an easy, symptom-free week.
  • Bad day or less than 6 h sleep: use the recovery-week switch, cap RPE at 6, and skip tests.
  • Illness: skip lifting with fever, chest symptoms, vomiting or systemic illness. Resume after recovery with 10% less load and half the sets; seek care for persistent or severe symptoms.
  • Early deload: after two poor sessions, persistent soreness, or an exam crunch, switch that week to recovery. Use half the work, 15% less load, RPE ≤6. Resume from the previous successful load; do not catch up.
  • Pain: stop the provoking movement. Use a pain-free substitute or skip it. Stop a substitute if symptoms appear. Arrange a clinician/physio assessment for ongoing back pain before heavy lower-lift tests.

Test weeks

Week 6: take one controlled single at RPE ≤8; then two easy triples. Week 12: use the listed single as the first attempt. If pain-free and RPE ≤7, optionally take +5 kg; if that is ≤8, optionally take another +5 kg. Rest 4–5 min. Stop at RPE 9, any pain, or a missed attempt. No repeat misses. Use bench spotters and squat safeties. Skip lower-lift testing unless pain has resolved and a clinician has advised that heavy lifting is appropriate.

Protect your sleep

Keep a steady bedtime. During exams, use recovery mode. Drink 2.5–3.5 L/day as a starting point; sip 400–800 mL/h during training, adjusting for heat and sweat. Do not force fluids.

Before training

2–3 hours before: eat 60–90 g carbohydrate and 25–40 g protein; keep fat and fibre modest. If needed 30–60 min before, take a banana or 20–30 g easy carbohydrate. Drink 400–600 mL over the 2 hours before training.

Optional supplements

Take creatine monohydrate 3–5 g every day; no loading needed. Keep intake consistent when tracking weight. Use a third-party batch-tested product. Check with your clinician first if you have kidney disease or take relevant medication. Creatine dosing ↗

Test-day caffeine: only use a dose already tolerated in training. Start with 100 mg; use up to 200 mg (~2 mg/kg) 45–60 min before lifting. Keep all-source intake ≤400 mg/day and avoid within 8 h of bedtime. Skip with palpitations, anxiety, uncontrolled blood pressure or a medical contraindication; ask a clinician about medication interactions. Do not use pure caffeine powder or stack stimulant products. MAKE IT YOURS

Set your rhythm.

Leave at least one full rest day between Day 1 Upper A and Day 3 Upper B. Train Day 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 in order. Leave 48 hours between lower sessions. Unassigned days remain flexible.

Back-pain check-in

Keep lower lifts in substitution mode until you have recorded the aggravating movements. Select the movement for each lower-body day, then confirm pain-free warm-ups that session. These checks do not diagnose or clear your back.

Stop lifting and seek emergency care for new bladder/bowel changes, saddle numbness, symptoms in both legs, chest pain, or pain after a serious accident. Seek urgent care for rapidly worsening severe pain or fever/unwellness. Back-pain actions ↗
